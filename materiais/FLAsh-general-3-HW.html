Class Content

Look up to

"Look up to" means to admire or respect someone because you think they are a good example, or because you want to be like them.
In the sentence "Her younger siblings look up to her", it means:
  • •The younger siblings respect and admire her.
  • •They may want to be like her or follow her example.
Synonym: Admire
Example: I admire my teacher because she is very kind and smart.

More Examples

I look up to my older brother because he works very hard.
(You admire your older brother and respect him because of his hard work.)
She looks up to famous scientists because she wants to become one.
(She admires famous scientists and respects them because she wants to follow their example.)

Take in

"Take in" means to accept or handle something, like tasks or information.
In "In her work, she was able to take in tasks at her own pace," it means:
  • •She could accept or handle the tasks without rushing.
  • •She controlled how many tasks she did at a time.
Synonym: Manage
Example: He manages his workload well by organizing his day.

More Examples

It was hard to take in all the information during the meeting.
(It was difficult to understand or absorb all the information shared in the meeting.)
He took in the instructions carefully before starting the project.
(He listened and understood the instructions carefully before beginning the project.)

Call Out

"Call Out" means to publicly criticize or point out someone's mistake or bad behavior openly.
In the sentence "She called out the company for bad service on social media," it means:
  • •She publicly criticized the company.
  • •She made others aware of the company's poor service.
  • •This happened on social media, where many people could see it.
Synonym: Expose
Example: The journalist exposed the corruption in the government.

More Examples

Activists call out unfair laws to demand change.
(Activists publicly criticize unfair laws to push for improvements or reforms.)
He called out his friend for lying to him.
(He confronted or challenged his friend directly because of the lie.)

Let's Practice

  • Is it possible to look up to someone you don't personally like? Why or why not?
  • Tell me about a time when you had to take in a lot of tasks or responsibilities. How did you manage?
  • Have you ever tried to be like someone you look up to? Describe your experience.
  • In your opinion, is it harder to take in academic information (from books) or emotional information (like a friend's problems)? Why?
  • Describe a situation where calling out a problem helped to solve it.

Self-reflection
